Ritik Kumar

BS-MS (Research) Student in Physics
Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru

I am a physics student and researcher interested in high-energy astrophysics, X-ray astronomy, and the physics of accreting supermassive black holes.

My research focuses on active galactic nuclei, X-ray coronae, accretion physics, and multi-phase outflows. I am also interested in machine learning and statistical methods for astrophysical data analysis.

Research

X-ray Coronae of Active Galactic Nuclei

I study the hot X-ray-emitting coronae surrounding supermassive black holes using X-ray spectroscopy, with a particular focus on their temperature, spectral properties, and connection to the accretion disk.

NuSTAR · X-ray spectroscopy · AGN · Accretion physics
Read more →

Multi-phase Outflows in Active Galactic Nuclei

I investigate outflows from active galactic nuclei across different gas phases using XMM-Newton and NuSTAR observations, including the study of ultra-fast outflows and multiple velocity components.

XMM-Newton · NuSTAR · AGN outflows · X-ray spectroscopy
Read more →

Gamma-Ray Burst Light-Curve Reconstruction

I work on reconstructing missing sections of GRB X-ray light curves using machine-learning and statistical approaches, including Deep Gaussian Processes, ReFANN, and physics-informed models.

GRBs · Machine Learning · Gaussian Processes · PINNs
Read more →

X-ray Polarization and Relativistic Reflection

I am investigating the geometry and physical properties of AGN accretion systems using X-ray polarization and relativistic reflection modelling, combining IXPE, XMM-Newton, and NuSTAR observations.

IXPE · XMM-Newton · NuSTAR · Relativistic reflection
