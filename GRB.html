Machine Learning for
Gamma-Ray Bursts

Developing uncertainty-aware machine-learning and probabilistic approaches for reconstructing irregularly sampled X-ray light curves of gamma-ray bursts.

The Problem

Gamma-ray burst X-ray light curves often contain temporal gaps, irregular sampling and observational uncertainties. Reconstructing the underlying temporal behaviour requires models that can learn complex patterns while quantifying the uncertainty of their predictions.

My work investigates several machine-learning and probabilistic approaches to this reconstruction problem. Rather than relying on a single architecture, I implemented and evaluated different modelling strategies and compared their predictive performance, uncertainty estimates and robustness.

Models & Methods

Each model addresses the reconstruction problem from a different quantitative perspective.

Model 01

Gaussian Processes

Gaussian Processes provide a probabilistic framework for modelling temporal data and reconstructing missing regions of a light curve while naturally representing uncertainty in the inferred function.

Model 02

Deep Gaussian Process

I implemented a two-layer Deep Gaussian Process in GPyTorch. Each Gaussian-process layer learns latent representations of the data, allowing the model to capture more complex temporal structure while retaining probabilistic outputs.

2 GP Layers GPyTorch RBF Kernel Variational Strategy Inducing Points
Model 03

ReFANN

ReFANN is a neural-network based reconstruction pipeline designed for irregularly sampled GRB light curves. The model was evaluated using quantitative error metrics and uncertainty estimates across a large GRB sample.

PyTorch Neural Network Heteroscedastic Output Regression
Model 04

Siamese Neural Network

I implemented a Siamese architecture using a shared encoder to learn representations from related GRB light-curve information. The learned representations were combined using query, reference, difference and Hadamard-product features.

Shared Encoder Reference Anchors Feature Fusion 128-D Representation

Siamese Architecture

A closer look at the representation-learning approach used in the reconstruction problem.

The Siamese network uses a shared encoder so that related inputs are transformed into a common representation space. The model combines the query and reference representations together with their difference and Hadamard product before producing a 128-dimensional representation.

The fusion uses the query representation, reference representation, their difference and their Hadamard product. The model also incorporates MC Dropout for uncertainty estimation and a physics-informed composite loss consisting of Gaussian negative log-likelihood and a monotonicity penalty.

Uncertainty & Validation

Reconstruction is only useful when the reliability of the inferred light curve can also be quantified.

MC Dropout & MCMC Sampling

I used dropout-based stochastic sampling to estimate predictive uncertainty and investigate the stability of the reconstructed light curves. Repeated stochastic forward passes were used to obtain distributions of model predictions rather than relying on a single deterministic estimate.

Bootstrap Resampling

Bootstrap resampling was used to assess the robustness of inferred quantities against variations in the sampled data.

Cross-Validation

ReFANN was evaluated using 5-fold cross-validation to test model performance across different subsets of the available observations.

Robust Statistics

Robust statistical methods included IQR-based uncertainty estimation and 3-sigma MAD-based outlier rejection.

Ablation Studies

Embedding dimensions of 16, 32 and 64 were compared to investigate the effect of representation size on model behaviour.

Confidence Intervals

95% confidence intervals were reported to quantify the statistical uncertainty associated with the reconstructed quantities.

Results

Quantitative results obtained from the implemented reconstruction pipelines.

Mean uncertainty reduction

ReFANN achieved a mean parameter uncertainty reduction of 62%, compared with 39% for the benchmark.

Test MSE

ReFANN achieved a test mean-squared error of 0.0196 in the evaluated reconstruction.

The Deep Gaussian Process reduced uncertainties in log Ta, log Fa and α by 11.6%, 12.3% and 15.9%, respectively.
