Ultra-Fast Outflows

Searching for powerful winds from supermassive black holes through systematic X-ray spectral analysis of a large quasar catalogue.

Why Ultra-Fast Outflows?

Ultra-fast outflows (UFOs) are highly ionised winds launched from the innermost regions of active galactic nuclei, with velocities reaching a significant fraction of the speed of light. Because they originate close to the central supermassive black hole, these winds provide a powerful probe of the connection between black-hole accretion, radiation and feedback on the surrounding galaxy.

Detecting and characterising UFOs allows us to investigate how energy and momentum are transferred from the accretion flow into the surrounding environment, and how these powerful winds may influence the evolution of their host galaxies.

From a Million Quasars to a Focused Sample

Rather than beginning with a small pre-selected sample, I developed a systematic search starting from the Million Quasars (Milliquas) catalogue. The goal was to identify previously unexplored sources with sufficient X-ray data to search for highly ionised absorption signatures associated with ultra-fast outflows.

Systematic Search Pipeline

The sample was progressively reduced using observational and spectroscopic criteria. This approach allows the final UFO candidates to be selected through a reproducible analysis rather than by subjective visual inspection alone.

1. Catalogue Cross-Matching

Starting from the Milliquas catalogue, I searched for corresponding archival X-ray observations and identified sources with suitable XMM-Newton data.

2. Sufficient Exposure

Sources were filtered according to the available exposure and data quality. Observations with insufficient statistics were removed because weak absorption features cannot be reliably distinguished from statistical fluctuations.

3. Archived Observation IDs

I retained sources with usable archived observation IDs so that the complete reduction and spectral-analysis pipeline could be reproduced from the publicly available X-ray data.

4. Continuum and Reflection Modelling

Candidate spectra were modelled using physically motivated continuum and reflection models. This step is important because reflection features can sometimes mimic or weaken apparent absorption signatures in the Fe-K band.

5. Outflow Signature Verification

A source was retained only when the absorption signature survived the inclusion of the appropriate reflection model, reducing the possibility that the candidate feature was produced by an incorrectly modelled continuum or reflection component.

6. Final UFO Candidates

Applying the complete selection and spectral-modelling procedure resulted in 26 final sources showing evidence for ultra-fast outflows and providing a sample for further detailed study.

Fe-K Absorption and Photoionisation Modelling

The Fe-K energy band provides an important window into highly ionised gas moving at high velocities. Blueshifted absorption from highly ionised iron can reveal the velocity, ionisation state and column density of the outflowing material.

I first establish a physically motivated description of the underlying X-ray continuum and reflection spectrum. Candidate absorption features are then tested against alternative spectral models to determine whether they remain statistically and physically significant.

For soft X-ray absorption features that could not be adequately described by the available zxipcf model, I developed custom XSTAR photoionisation grids. I also developed XSTAR grids for modelling highly ionised absorption associated with Fe-K features.

Key idea: reflection modelling is an essential part of the UFO search because an incorrectly modelled reflection spectrum can produce or hide apparent absorption features. The outflow candidates are therefore required to survive the full spectral modelling procedure.

What Can These Outflows Tell Us?

Measuring the velocity, ionisation and column density of the outflowing gas allows us to estimate the physical properties of the wind and investigate its connection with the central accretion flow.

The inferred outflow properties can be used to study the kinetic power and momentum carried by the wind, providing insight into whether AGN-driven outflows can transfer sufficient energy into their host galaxies to influence their evolution.

The final sample therefore provides an opportunity to study UFOs systematically rather than as isolated detections, and to search for common relationships between outflow properties and the underlying properties of the active galactic nucleus.

Computational and Statistical Analysis

This project combines large-scale catalogue processing with automated data reduction, X-ray spectral modelling and statistical evaluation. I developed Python-based workflows to search and filter large astronomical datasets and to organise the subsequent spectral analysis.

The analysis requires distinguishing genuine physical absorption features from statistical fluctuations and modelling uncertainties. This makes robust model comparison, parameter estimation and reproducible computational workflows central to the analysis.
