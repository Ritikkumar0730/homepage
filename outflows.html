Ultra Fast Outflows

Searching for High-Velocity Outflows in Active Galactic Nuclei

Research Overview

My research investigates ultra fast outflows in active galactic nuclei using archival XMM-Newton observations. These highly ionised winds can carry material away from the central regions of a galaxy at a substantial fraction of the speed of light.

I developed a systematic, Python-based workflow to search for and analyse absorption signatures associated with high-velocity outflows in large X-ray datasets.

The project combines large-scale data processing, automated source selection, X-ray spectral modelling, photoionisation modelling and statistical testing to identify and characterise candidate ultra fast outflows.

Large-Scale Data Analysis

I built a Python pipeline to cross-match a large AGN catalogue with the XMM-Newton archive and systematically select observations suitable for high-quality spectral analysis.

  • Cross-matching large astronomical catalogues with XMM-Newton observations
  • Automated observation and source selection
  • X-ray data reduction and spectral extraction
  • Signal-to-noise and count-based quality filtering
  • Parallel processing for large observational datasets

Searching for Outflows

The analysis focuses on identifying absorption features in the X-ray spectra that can indicate highly ionised gas moving at relativistic velocities.

I use spectral modelling to establish the underlying continuum and then investigate statistically significant absorption signatures. Candidate features are examined across the relevant energy range and tested against alternative spectral models.

Photoionisation Modelling

A major part of the analysis involves modelling the physical properties of the absorbing gas. I developed custom XSTAR photoionisation grids when existing publicly available models were not sufficient to reproduce the observed soft X-ray signatures.

The grids allow the absorption spectrum to be explored as a function of physical parameters such as ionisation state, column density and turbulent velocity.

Statistical Analysis

Candidate absorption features are evaluated using statistical model comparison and parameter estimation. I use χ² fitting, simulations and uncertainty analysis to assess whether additional spectral components are supported by the data.

For parameter inference in more complex models, I also use Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods to explore posterior parameter distributions, correlations and degeneracies.

Key Result

In one of the luminous quasars studied, I identified evidence for multi-phase ultra fast outflows with inferred velocities of approximately 0.22c and 0.32c.

This work required combining automated data analysis with detailed physical modelling and independent validation of the detected absorption features.

Research and Manuscript

This work is being developed into a manuscript for submission to the Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society (MNRAS). The project involves collaboration with researchers working on X-ray spectroscopy and active galactic nuclei.
