Coronae

X-ray Coronae Around Supermassive Black Holes

Research Overview

I study the hot X-ray emitting coronae surrounding supermassive black holes using multi-epoch NuSTAR and XMM-Newton observations. My work focuses on understanding the physical properties of these compact regions through broadband X-ray spectral analysis.

I work with observational datasets from multiple epochs and use Python-based data reduction and analysis together with X-ray spectral modelling to investigate variations in the coronal properties of active galactic nuclei.

The central goal is to use quantitative analysis of X-ray observations to constrain the physical conditions of the corona and understand how these properties evolve across different observational epochs.

Data and Analysis

My analysis involves working with multi-epoch X-ray observations from NuSTAR and XMM-Newton. The workflow includes data reduction, background filtering, spectral extraction, grouping and broadband spectral modelling.

  • Multi-epoch NuSTAR observations
  • XMM-Newton observations
  • Python-based data processing and analysis
  • X-ray spectral modelling using XSPEC
  • Statistical comparison of competing models
  • Parameter estimation and uncertainty analysis

Coronal Temperature

One of my research directions is constraining the temperature of the hot X-ray corona using broadband X-ray observations. I analyse the high-energy X-ray spectrum to investigate the spectral cutoff and infer the physical properties of the Comptonising corona.

By comparing multiple observations of the same source, I investigate whether the inferred coronal properties remain stable or vary between different epochs.

Statistical Modelling

The analysis relies on quantitative model fitting and statistical inference. I use parameter estimation, uncertainty analysis and model comparison to determine which physical models provide an adequate description of the observed data.

For more complex models, I also use Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods to explore parameter distributions and investigate correlations and degeneracies between model parameters.

Computational Research

These projects require processing large observational datasets and maintaining reproducible analysis workflows. I use Python, Linux-based environments and high-performance computing resources for data processing, modelling and statistical analysis.
