A Systematic Search for Ultra-Fast Outflows in Active Galactic Nuclei and Multi-Phase outflows study of a Quasar Source.

Ritik Kumar C. S. Stalin James N. Reeves

This work involves the systematic X-ray spectroscopy study of milli quas catalog. In this work, XMM-Newton data has been used for to capture outflows signature and other features such as warm absorber, soft excess, and reflection festures. The purpose of this work is include more sources in the list of specific category based on the nature of particular AGN showing and then detailed study of AGN. We have found after the spectral study of 473 sources, many of them are showing highly warm absorber signature, some showing soft excess, some showing strong relativistic effects, some showing the galactic emission that usually lies in 2-4 keV energy band, and some showing evidence for outflows including the forest-tree (more than one outflows). We have complete list of sources and made the different buckets. We also, did the detailed study of some AGN those were showing outflows evidence and studies about those have not been done yet. (I am expecting to explores these in more details while combining it with other data in order to see the full picture.) The outflows are also important to see how it is affecting the AGN feedback and how it is affecting the AGN evolution. It is also help in to study how the central engine evolve with time and how the corona-disk-jet connection is working. Then it will leads to how the timing properties can tells us more about the accretion disk and how the corona is working. Since the time for bachelor's thesis was 8 months, therefore, we have choosen one source that source has not studied in detailed earlier, and one of the particular highlight of this is this source has two different knietics outflows which is part of single outflow, we call it as stratified outflows, and these two outflows persist between two observations. We also found this source has the flux variability between the two observations and also within single observation using timing analysis. Later, we estimated the location and energetics of both UFO components and found that these outflows are powerful enough to provide the AGN feedback. Work on this is under peer review.

Research Overview

What the Thesis Addresses

  • Systematic study of the Milli-Quas catalog with XMM-Newton data: spectral analysis of 473 sources, categorised by spectral features shown in their EPIC-pn spectra.
  • Detailed spectral study of 30 sources exhibiting outflows (blue-shifted Fe XXV/XXVI absorption lines) that have not been studied in this context before.
  • Highly detailed spectral and timing analysis of a quasar observed across two epochs, revealing two stratified UFO components that persist between observations.
  • Estimation of outflow location and energetics, and assessment of how these winds influence the surrounding environment and host galaxy.
  • Analysis uses both EPIC-MOS1/2 and RGS1/2 data throughout.

Where This Leads

Next Directions

  • Spectral and timing analysis of remaining 29 sources of having outflows and other interesting features.
  • We found two layers of the wind moving at different speeds. The natural question now is — what actually pushes this gas out? Is it light pressure from the disk, magnetic fields, or both? That connects straight into accretion disk physics.
  • We detected a wind in a redshift range where almost nothing had been found before. This opens the question of how these winds change over cosmic time — were they stronger when galaxies were forming the fastest?
  • The wind carries about 5–10% of the black hole’s total radiation as kinetic energy, which is right at the threshold where it could be slowing down star formation in the host galaxy. The next step is actually testing whether it does.
  • The black hole in our source is eating faster than it theoretically should — what we call super-Eddington. Understanding how winds behave in this extreme regime matters a lot for how the biggest black holes grew in the early universe.
  • We see a fast hot wind and a slower cooler absorber in the same source at the same time. The question is whether these are just different layers of one single wind, and how energy moves from the small disk scale all the way out to galaxy-wide molecular clouds.
  • The wind barely changed across 6 months of observations, which is actually surprising. What happens on shorter timescales — days or weeks? Can the wind just switch off if the black hole suddenly gets brighter or dimmer?
