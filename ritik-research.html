A Systematic Search for Ultra-Fast Outflows in Active Galactic Nuclei and Multi-Phase outflows study of a Quasar Source.

This work involves the systematic X-ray spectroscopy study of milli quas catalog. In this work, XMM-Newton data has been used for to capture outflows signature and other features such as warm absorber, soft excess, and reflection festures. The purpose of this work is include more sources in the list of specific category based on the nature of particular AGN showing and then detailed study of AGN. We have found after the spectral study of 473 sources, many of them are showing highly warm absorber signature, some showing soft excess, some showing strong relativistic effects, some showing the galactic emission that usually lies in 2-4 keV energy band, and some showing evidence for outflows including the forest-tree (more than one outflows). We have complete list of sources and made the different buckets. We also, did the detailed study of some AGN those were showing outflows evidence and studies about those have not been done yet. (I am expecting to explores these in more details while combining it with other data in order to see the full picture.) These winds are highly important to see how it is influencing the host galaxy and how it is affecting the star formation in the host galaxy. The outflows are also important to see how it is affecting the black hole growth and how it is affecting the accretion disk. The outflows are also important to see how it is affecting the AGN feedback and how it is affecting the AGN evolution. It is also help in to study how the central engine evolve with time and how the corona-disk-jet connection is working. Then it will leads to how the timing properties can tells us more about the accretion disk and how the corona is working. Since the time for bachelor's thesis was 8 months, therefore, we have choosen one source that source has not studied in detailed, and one of the particular highlight of this is this source has two different knietics outflows which is part of single outflow, we call it as stratified outflows, and these two outflows persist between two observations. We also found this source has the flux variability between the two observations and also within single observation using timing analysis. Later, we estimated the location and energetics of both UFO components and found that these outflows are powerful enough to provide the AGN feedback. Work on this is under peer review.

Research Overview

What the Thesis Addresses

  • Systematic study of the Milli-Quas catalog with XMM-Newton data: spectral analysis of 473 sources, categorised by spectral features shown in their EPIC-pn spectra.
  • Detailed spectral study of 26 sources exhibiting outflows (blue-shifted Fe XXV/XXVI absorption lines) that have not been studied in this context before.
  • Highly detailed spectral and timing analysis of a quasar observed across two epochs, revealing two stratified UFO components that persist between observations.
  • Estimation of outflow location and energetics, and assessment of how these winds influence the surrounding environment and host galaxy.
  • Analysis uses both EPIC-MOS1/2 and RGS1/2 data throughout.
⚡
Ultra-fast Outflows · Hard X-ray Phase
Highly ionized Fe XXV/XXVI absorption at 7–10 keV, velocities up to 0.4c. Modeled with XSPEC and custom XSTAR photoionization grids. Detected in both individual sources and the systematic survey.
🌊
Warm Absorbers · Soft X-ray Phase
Lower-ionization outflow gas traced with RGS spectroscopy in the 0.3–2 keV band. Simultaneous with EPIC-pn data, enabling a direct comparison of high- and low-ionization wind components in the same AGN epoch.
🌌
Feedback Connection · Multi-wavelength
JWST infrared observations place X-ray wind measurements in the broader feedback context, connecting nuclear outflow properties to host-galaxy emission on larger spatial scales.
Key Sources Studied

Individual AGN Precision Analysis

MCG−6−30−15
Canonical Warm Absorber · Seyfert 1.2
The benchmark AGN for X-ray warm absorber and UFO studies. With 12 XMM-Newton observations spanning 2000–2024, MCG−6−30−15 provides an unmatched 24-year temporal baseline. Flux-resolved spectral analysis reveals how UFO ionization state and column density respond to changes in the ionizing luminosity — a critical test for discriminating between models of variable covering fraction and variable ionization.
12 observations 2000–2024 Flux-resolved EPIC-pn + RGS Warm absorber
1H 0707−495
Extreme Variability · Narrow-Line Seyfert 1
One of the most spectacularly variable AGN known, with a soft X-ray spectrum that drops steeply above 7 keV. This source tests competing physical models — ionized reflection versus complex warm absorption — in a high-accretion-rate NLS1 regime. Multi-epoch analysis probes whether the extreme spectral variability is driven by changes in the absorbing column or in the reflected fraction.
NLS1 High variability Reflection vs absorption High accretion rate
PG 1126−041
Confirmed UFO Host · Radio-quiet Quasar
A radio-quiet quasar with a well-characterized ultra-fast outflow, used as a precision benchmark for XSTAR photoionization modeling of Fe XXV/XXVI absorption. Detailed spectral fitting constrains log ξ, NH, and vout for the absorbing gas, providing a calibration point for the systematic survey pipeline.

Analysis Pipeline

How the Research Was Done

M · 01
XMM-Newton Data Reduction
Full EPIC-pn and RGS reduction using XMM-Newton SAS. Event filtering (pattern ≤ 4), source and background region extraction, good time interval screening for soft proton flares, and exposure-weighted stacking for multi-epoch observations.
M · 02
XSPEC Spectral Fitting
Multi-component spectral modeling: power-law and Comptonization continua, ionized reflection (pexrav, relxill), neutral and ionized absorbers. χ² and C-statistic minimization with Monte Carlo confidence intervals using Markov chains.
M · 03
XSTAR Photoionization Grids
Custom photoionization models computed with XSTAR for each source's spectral energy distribution. Grids spanning log ξ, NH, and outflow velocity to characterize the physical state of the absorbing gas beyond simple Gaussian fitting.
M · 04
Flux-Resolved Spectroscopy
Observations sorted by source flux state to track how absorption properties evolve with accretion luminosity. This method — applied to 24 years of MCG−6−30−15 data — is critical for separating intrinsic absorber variability from ionization-driven responses.
M · 05
Systematic UFO Survey (473 Sources)
Automated spectral pipeline deployed across 473 EPIC-pn AGN observations in the XMM-Newton archive. Blind search for blue-shifted Fe absorption at ≥3σ significance, with Forest-UFO statistical treatment to control for multiple spectral feature detections.
M · 06
EPIC-pn Stacking Analysis
Co-added spectra for sources below individual detection threshold, enabling constraints on mean outflow properties in AGN sub-populations. Weighted stacking preserving spectral resolution and S/N, with careful treatment of systematic effects from combining non-simultaneous observations.
Scientific Contributions

What This Research Contributes

📡
The Largest XMM-Newton UFO Census to Date
A systematic, homogeneous search for ultra-fast outflows across 473 AGN in the EPIC-pn archive — building a statistical foundation for understanding UFO occurrence rates, velocity and ionization distributions, and their dependence on AGN luminosity, accretion rate, and black hole mass.
🔗
Multi-phase Outflow Connection in Individual AGN
Simultaneous EPIC-pn + RGS analysis directly connects the high-ionization UFO phase to the lower-ionization warm absorber in the same source and observational epoch, placing physical constraints on whether these phases represent a single outflow at different temperatures or kinematically distinct wind components.
📊
Flux-State Dependence of UFO Properties in MCG−6−30−15
24 years of XMM-Newton monitoring reveals how the warm absorber and UFO respond to changes in the ionizing luminosity. Flux-resolved spectroscopy across 12 epochs probes the physical mechanism driving absorption variability and provides constraints on the distance and geometry of the outflowing gas.
🔭
Multi-wavelength Context with JWST
JWST infrared analysis connects X-ray wind measurements to the host-galaxy scale, situating the nuclear outflow properties within the broader AGN feedback framework. This multi-wavelength approach provides observational context for models linking UFO kinetic energy to large-scale molecular outflow driving.
