What is Quantitative Research?

Quantitative research is the process of using mathematics, statistics, computation and data to formulate models, test hypotheses and make decisions under uncertainty.

In quantitative finance, these ideas are applied to financial markets: market data are transformed into measurable signals, statistical models are used to understand patterns and uncertainty, and computational methods are used to test and implement strategies.

My interest in quantitative research comes from the same scientific approach I use in astrophysics: formulate a problem mathematically, build a model, test it against data, quantify uncertainty and examine how robust the result is.

Probability & Statistical Inference

Probability provides the mathematical framework for reasoning when the outcome of a process is uncertain. Statistical inference then allows us to learn about an underlying process from observed data.

Inference

I work with likelihood-based inference, uncertainty estimation, hypothesis testing, resampling and statistical model comparison.

Uncertainty

Rather than treating a model prediction as a single number, I am interested in understanding the distribution and uncertainty associated with the prediction.

Monte Carlo & MCMC

Monte Carlo methods provide a powerful way to study complicated probabilistic systems through repeated random sampling. I have used Monte Carlo methods for uncertainty estimation and computational inference.

I have also worked with Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods to explore parameter distributions and quantify uncertainties in model parameters.

Why this matters

The central idea is particularly relevant to quantitative modelling: instead of asking only for the best-fitting value, we can investigate the range of parameter values supported by the data and the correlations between them.

Optimisation & Model Selection

Many quantitative problems ultimately require finding parameters or strategies that optimise an objective while accounting for uncertainty and constraints.

My computational work has involved numerical optimisation, parameter estimation, model comparison and validation. I am particularly interested in understanding when an apparent improvement in a model represents a genuine improvement rather than overfitting.

Why Quantitative Finance?

Although my research background is in physics and astrophysics, the underlying problem-solving process is highly quantitative.

Scientific Data → Market Data

Both require cleaning, filtering, transforming and validating large datasets before reliable conclusions can be drawn.

Physical Models → Statistical Models

Both involve constructing models, estimating parameters and testing whether the model explains the observed data.

Measurement Uncertainty → Risk

Understanding uncertainty is central to both scientific inference and decision-making under financial uncertainty.

Simulation → Strategy Testing

Monte Carlo simulation and computational experiments provide a framework for testing hypotheses under controlled conditions.
