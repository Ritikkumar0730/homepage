Quantitative & Computational Research

Statistical Modelling, Scientific Computing, Data Analysis and Machine Learning

Research Approach

My research involves building computational methods to work with large, noisy and complex datasets. Across my projects, I combine statistical modelling, numerical methods, machine learning and scientific computing to turn raw data into reproducible results.

I enjoy problems where the underlying signal is difficult to isolate, the data are imperfect, and the model has to be tested carefully rather than accepted from a single result.

My computational work follows a research cycle of data preparation, hypothesis formation, model development, parameter estimation, validation, uncertainty analysis and reproducible evaluation.

Large-Scale Data Analysis

I have developed Python pipelines for processing large observational datasets, including catalogue cross-matching, automated source selection, quality filtering, data reduction and multi-epoch analysis.

I use vectorised numerical operations, structured data processing and parallel computation when appropriate to make research workflows efficient and reproducible.

  • Large catalogue cross-matching and data selection
  • Automated data-processing pipelines
  • Multi-epoch and multi-instrument datasets
  • Parallel processing and HPC workflows
  • Quality control and signal-to-noise based filtering

Statistical Modelling

I use statistical modelling to estimate parameters, compare competing hypotheses and quantify uncertainty. My experience includes likelihood-based fitting, χ² model comparison, simulation-based testing and probabilistic modelling.

I also use Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods to explore parameter distributions, correlations and degeneracies in models with multiple interacting parameters.

Time-Series Analysis

Working with transient astronomical observations has given me extensive experience with time-dependent and irregularly sampled data. I analyse temporal structure, missing observations, measurement noise and changing signal behaviour.

In my machine-learning work, I developed methods for reconstructing noisy and irregularly sampled GRB X-ray light curves using Gaussian-process based and neural-network approaches.

Machine Learning

My machine-learning work includes Deep Gaussian Processes, neural networks and Physics-Informed Neural Networks implemented in PyTorch. I use machine learning as a modelling tool while maintaining emphasis on validation, uncertainty and interpretation.

I have worked with cross-validation, bootstrap resampling, uncertainty estimation and quantitative error analysis to test whether model performance is robust to changes in the data.

Numerical Methods

I have implemented and compared numerical methods for solving differential equations, including Runge-Kutta and finite difference approaches alongside Physics-Informed Neural Networks.

These projects required attention to numerical stability, discretisation, convergence and the effect of computational parameters on the final solution.

Model Validation and Robustness

I place particular emphasis on checking whether a result is genuinely supported by the data. Depending on the problem, I use cross-validation, bootstrap resampling, simulations, parameter uncertainty, model comparison and controlled numerical tests.

I also examine failure modes and parameter degeneracies rather than focusing only on the final performance metric.

This approach is important to me because a model that performs well on one dataset or one experiment is not necessarily a robust model. I therefore try to understand how sensitive a result is to the data, assumptions, parameters and validation procedure.

Computational Research at Scale

I have worked in Linux and HPC environments for computational research, including processing multi-epoch datasets and running parameter-intensive analyses. I have experience structuring workflows so that data preparation, modelling and statistical analysis can be repeated systematically.

My research code is primarily written in Python, with scientific computing libraries used for numerical analysis, data handling and machine learning.

Why Quantitative Research

I am interested in quantitative research because it combines mathematical reasoning, statistics, programming and empirical testing. I particularly enjoy taking an open-ended problem, deciding what can actually be measured from the available data, building a model and then testing whether the evidence supports the hypothesis.

My current research has trained me to work with noisy data, uncertainty, complex models and computational experiments. I want to apply these skills to new data-driven problems and continue developing my understanding of statistical modelling and quantitative decision-making.

Probability, Statistics and Inference

My quantitative coursework and research have given me a strong foundation in probability, statistics, linear algebra, multivariable calculus, optimisation and statistical inference. I use these ideas when deciding how to formulate a model, estimate parameters and evaluate whether an observed effect is statistically meaningful.

In research, I work with likelihood-based fitting, uncertainty propagation, correlation analysis, Monte Carlo simulations, bootstrap resampling and posterior parameter distributions. I pay particular attention to uncertainty and to the difference between a model fitting the observed data and a model being genuinely supported by the evidence.

Quantitative focus: probability, statistical inference, parameter estimation, hypothesis testing, uncertainty quantification, Monte Carlo methods, bootstrap methods and model comparison.

Optimisation and Parameter Estimation

Many of my projects involve estimating several interacting parameters from noisy observations. I have worked with numerical optimisation, likelihood-based fitting and MCMC sampling to explore parameter spaces, uncertainties and degeneracies.

I am interested in optimisation not only as a mathematical problem but also as a practical research problem: choosing a suitable objective, understanding parameter sensitivity, checking convergence and testing whether the resulting solution is stable.

This has trained me to think carefully about detection thresholds, selection effects, false positives, model assumptions and the robustness of a signal under changes in the data or analysis procedure.

Feature Engineering and Data Preparation

I have built research pipelines in which raw observations first have to be cleaned, filtered, transformed and structured before statistical or machine-learning models can be applied. This includes catalogue cross-matching, quality cuts, signal-to-noise filtering, feature construction and automated data selection.

I consider data preparation part of the modelling process. Poor data quality, leakage, selection effects or inconsistent preprocessing can produce apparently strong results that do not generalise.

Generalisation, Overfitting and Validation

I use validation as a central part of model development. Depending on the problem, I use held-out data, cross-validation, bootstrap resampling, simulations and sensitivity analysis to test whether a result survives reasonable changes in the experimental setup.

In machine-learning projects, I have worked with noisy and irregularly sampled data where overfitting can be particularly important. I compare model behaviour rather than relying on a single training metric.

Research Pipeline

I generally approach a computational research problem through the following sequence:

1. Problem Define the measurable question and assumptions.
2. Data Collect, clean, filter and structure the dataset.
3. Hypothesis Develop a quantitative explanation or testable idea.
4. Model Build a statistical, numerical or ML model.
5. Validation Test generalisation, uncertainty and robustness.
6. Interpretation Understand the result, limitations and failure modes.

From Research to Quantitative Finance

I am particularly interested in applying this research framework to systematic quantitative problems. The parts I find most compelling are hypothesis generation, large-scale data analysis, statistical signal discovery, model validation and understanding whether an apparent pattern remains robust outside the data used to discover it.

I am actively developing my understanding of financial time series, systematic investing, market microstructure, portfolio construction, risk and backtesting. I do not present my scientific projects as financial trading experience; instead, I see them as a strong foundation in the research process required to work on quantitative problems.
